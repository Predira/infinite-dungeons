**Infinite Dungeons** was designed and programmed by **Claude**, Anthropic's AI model: the engine, the rules, the crypt and its monsters, Toadstool Hollow, the interface… Claude also made the original pictures on this page from the game itself.

- **Music** made with Suno and ElevenLabs.
- **Sound effects** made with ElevenLabs, or synthesized by the game from recipes.
- **Fonts** Lilita One and Nunito (SIL Open Font License), 3D with **three.js** (MIT License).
- **Code under the MIT License**. The music, sound effects and fonts keep their own rights: it's all listed in CREDITS.md.

The engine reads all of its content as **data**: heroes, weapons, powers, monsters, rooms, universes. That's what a **pack** is made of, and new worlds and equipment reach the dungeon the same way.

**The Sunken Forge** is a whole second universe in one pack: its rooms, monsters, bosses, two heroes, weapons and cards are all data. It was built by **Codex**, an AI coding agent, from a brief written by Claude, along with the engine pieces it needed (among them a universe per stratum, timed hazards and sparks that jump), and Claude reviewed it batch by batch. It's on from the start; you can switch it off in **Options**, under **Packs**, then restart.

**Toadstool Hollow**, the third universe, is a pack as well: six creatures, the Toad King, eleven rooms with their own decor, four weapons, two new rarities, cards and treasures. Claude wrote its brief and built it with a team of its own agents, each piece looked over by a second one, then measured on thousands of runs played by robots. The same night, the Bone Colossus, the Ossuary Warden, The Hammerer and The Forgemaster were rebuilt round, with faces. It's on from the start too.

**Volte**, a hero made for touch screens, was built by Codex too, from Claude's brief, with the glide and the back strike she needed, and Claude reviewed it batch by batch. She's off by default: switch her on in the same place.

For the twenty-stratum campaign, Codex helped bring the **Powder Magazine** laboratory, **Stillwater Bog**, **Rock Bottom** and the final gates into play. **Gemini** prototyped three weapons you'll find along the way: the Cast-Iron Skillet, the Gust Bellows and the Pinball Trombone.

Codex built **Sledge** and his quarry hammer, the bilingual **Field guide** and the new Duo signals. For Sledge, recordings of rushing air, struck steel and cracking stone from the game's ElevenLabs library were cut and mixed to follow the swing.

Codex also built the **Ferrywoman**, **Bell Ringer** and **Seamstress**. Their tools move with their hands, from the angle of a loaded boat hook to the wrist lifting the next clapper. Their sound cues are synthesized by the game.

For 1.14, Claude asked for builds that matter deep down. **Codex** built the card families and their tiers, the six difficulties and the auto-aim for every device, then tuned the descent on hundreds of runs played by two kinds of robot: one that picks the first card it sees, one that builds. **Gemini** wrote and drew the eighteen new cards, and Codex reviewed them.

The **Demo Pack** is the small one, without a single line of code. It's off by default: switch it on in the same place.
